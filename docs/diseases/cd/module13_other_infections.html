This closing module of the Selected Communicable Diseases series gathers the remaining NMC-listed infections that don’t individually warrant a full module — most already prevented by the same Universal Immunization Programme schedule introduced in Chapter 14, or controlled by the same water/sanitation and vector principles running throughout this series.

Measles, Rubella, and Mumps

🟢 MUST KNOW

  • Measles — a highly contagious paramyxovirus, spread by respiratory droplets; classic triad of fever, cough/coryza/ conjunctivitis, followed by a characteristic descending rash; complications (pneumonia, encephalitis) drive most measles deaths, especially in malnourished children
  • Rubella — usually mild in children, but infection during early pregnancy can cause Congenital Rubella Syndrome (CRS) — deafness, cataracts, heart defects — making rubella control a maternal/child health priority, not just a childhood-disease one
  • Mumps — parotid gland swelling; can rarely cause orchitis, meningitis, or pancreatitis

India’s MR elimination goal: the MR vaccine (combined measles-rubella) is included in UIP specifically to interrupt transmission of both viruses together, since a single vaccine addresses two distinct public health problems (acute measles disease burden and CRS prevention) at once.

Diphtheria, Pertussis, and Meningococcal Meningitis

🟡 SHOULD KNOW

Disease Agent Key feature
Diphtheria Corynebacterium diphtheriae Toxin-mediated; classic pseudomembrane in the throat can cause airway obstruction
Pertussis (whooping cough) Bordetella pertussis Paroxysmal coughing fits with a characteristic “whoop”; most dangerous in young infants
Meningococcal meningitis Neisseria meningitidis Can progress to death within hours; close-contact chemoprophylaxis is used to prevent secondary cases

Shared control strategy: diphtheria and pertussis are both prevented by the pentavalent vaccine already introduced in Chapter 14’s immunization content — their grouping here is a reminder that a single UIP vaccine often prevents multiple diseases at once (DPT covers three).

ARI and SAANS: acute respiratory infections remain a leading cause of under-five mortality — India’s SAANS initiative (Social Awareness and Action to Neutralize Pneumonia Successfully) specifically targets childhood pneumonia reduction, extending the Under-Five Clinic’s curative-care role (Chapter 14) to this single leading cause.

Influenza and COVID-19

🟡 SHOULD KNOW

Both illustrate pandemic potential in a respiratory virus — the same International Health Regulations and PHEIC framework (Chapter 26) exists largely because of diseases like these:

  • Influenza — seasonal strains circulate yearly; avian influenza (e.g. H5N1) and H1N1 (“swine flu”) represent zoonotic spillover and pandemic strains with the potential to spread efficiently between humans, unlike typical seasonal strains
  • COVID-19 / SARS — coronaviruses causing severe acute respiratory syndrome; COVID-19’s 2020 declaration as a PHEIC is the most recent, largest-scale real-world example of the PHEIC mechanism described in Chapter 26, directly connecting that abstract legal framework to a pandemic every reader lived through

Why these are grouped as a pair: both demonstrate how a novel or zoonotic respiratory virus can escalate from a local cluster to a global health emergency, which is exactly the scenario IHR (2005) and its PHEIC mechanism were designed to manage.

Neonatal Tetanus Elimination

🟢 MUST KNOW

Agent: Clostridium tetani, a spore-forming bacterium producing a potent neurotoxin; spores enter through wound contamination — classically, unhygienic cutting/care of the umbilical cord in newborns causes neonatal tetanus.

  • Maternal and Neonatal Tetanus Elimination (MNTE) — prevented by maternal tetanus toxoid vaccination during pregnancy (passive antibody transfer to the newborn) combined with clean delivery and cord-care practices
  • India was validated as having eliminated maternal and neonatal tetanus as a public health problem — verified using the same “elimination as a public health problem” threshold logic (not literal zero cases) already seen for filariasis, kala-azar, and leprosy

Why it belongs alongside RCH content: MNTE is as much a maternal/ newborn care success (Chapter 14’s antenatal and essential newborn care content) as it is a vaccine-preventable disease story.

Neglected Infections: A Reference Table

🟡 SHOULD KNOW

Lower-burden today, but still named in India’s communicable disease surveillance and worth recognizing:

Disease Agent/cause Key feature
Trachoma Chlamydia trachomatis Chronic conjunctival infection; repeated infection can cause blindness — a preventable cause alongside those in the Blindness module
Yaws Treponema pallidum pertenue A skin treponematosis (non-venereal); India was certified yaws-free in 2016
Taeniasis / hydatid disease Taenia spp. / Echinococcus Acquired via undercooked pork/beef (taeniasis) or contact with infected dogs (hydatid) — food/animal-hygiene diseases
Rickettsial disease (scrub typhus) Orientia tsutsugamushi Transmitted by chigger mite bites; classic eschar (a black, crusted skin lesion) at the bite site aids diagnosis

Why these still matter for an exam, if not for daily practice everywhere: each represents a distinct transmission principle (ocular contact, congenital/direct contact, foodborne parasite, mite-borne rickettsia) worth being able to name even where prevalence is now low.
