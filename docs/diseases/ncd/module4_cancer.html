Cancer I - patterns and causes

Patterns and Causes

🟢 MUST KNOW

Cancer site patterns in India differ meaningfully from high-income countries, reflecting different exposure patterns:

  • Men: oral cavity/lung cancers rank highest, strongly linked to tobacco use (smoked and smokeless)
  • Women: breast cancer has overtaken cervical cancer as the most common site in urban India, while cervical cancer remains far more prominent in rural/lower-resource settings — a pattern tracking access to screening and HPV exposure, not biology alone
  • Modifiable risk factors: tobacco (the single largest attributable cause worldwide), alcohol, poor diet, physical inactivity, and chronic infections (HPV for cervical cancer, hepatitis B/C for liver cancer — recall the viral hepatitis module)
  • Non-modifiable factors: age, genetic predisposition, family history

Levels of Prevention Applied to Cancer

🟢 MUST KNOW

Chapter 2’s prevention framework maps directly onto cancer control:

Level Example
Primary Tobacco control, HPV vaccination, hepatitis B vaccination, dietary modification
Secondary Screening — Pap smear/VIA for cervical cancer, mammography for breast cancer, oral visual examination
Tertiary Treatment (surgery, chemotherapy, radiotherapy), palliative care for advanced disease

Why cancer especially rewards a multi-level approach: unlike an acute infection with one clear intervention point, cancer’s long latency between exposure and disease means primary prevention (removing the exposure decades earlier) and secondary prevention (catching it early once it starts) both meaningfully change outcomes — neither alone is sufficient.

Cancer II - screening and control, NCRP and HPV vaccination

National Cancer Registry Programme

🟡 SHOULD KNOW

The National Cancer Registry Programme (NCRP), run by the Indian Council of Medical Research (ICMR), is India’s system for tracking cancer patterns:

  • Population-Based Cancer Registries (PBCR) — capture all cancer cases in a defined geographic population, enabling true incidence rate calculation
  • Hospital-Based Cancer Registries (HBCR) — capture cases seen at a specific hospital, useful for clinical/treatment-pattern data but cannot generate population incidence rates (recall the numerator/ denominator logic from Chapter 3 — a hospital’s catchment population is not well-defined)

Why this data matters: cancer control priorities (which cancers to screen for, where to focus tobacco control) are set using exactly this registry data — without it, prevention efforts would be guesswork.

HPV Vaccination and Cervical Cancer Prevention

🟡 SHOULD KNOW

Human papillomavirus (HPV) causes the large majority of cervical cancer cases — making cervical cancer one of the few cancers with a primary-prevention vaccine, not just secondary-prevention screening.

  • HPV vaccine — most effective when given before sexual debut (typically ages 9–14), since it prevents initial infection rather than treating existing infection
  • Complementary, not competing, with screening: vaccination prevents infection in the vaccinated cohort, but cervical cancer screening (Pap smear/VIA) remains necessary for unvaccinated women and for HPV types not covered by the vaccine
  • India has moved toward including HPV vaccination in its national immunization efforts, extending the same primary-prevention logic already used for hepatitis B (also cancer-preventing, via liver cancer) to a second cancer-causing infection
