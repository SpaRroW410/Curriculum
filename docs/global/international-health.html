Evolution of International Health Organizations

🟢 MUST KNOW

WHO did not emerge in isolation — it consolidated a century of earlier, fragmented efforts:

International Sanitary Conferences (from 1851, Paris — quarantine coordination, no lasting agreement) → Pan American Sanitary Bureau (1902, the world's first international health agency) → Office International d'Hygiène Publique (1907, Paris — communicable disease intelligence) → Health Organization of the League of Nations (1923) → UNRRA (1943) → WHO (1948)

Why this history matters: each predecessor contributed something WHO still uses — the Pan American Sanitary Bureau became PAHO, WHO’s regional office for the Americas; the League’s Health Organization pioneered the expert-committee model and periodic epidemiological reporting that WHO still runs today.

WHO Governance, Revisited

🟢 MUST KNOW

Building on Chapter 1’s brief introduction:

  • World Health Assembly — WHO’s supreme decision-making body, composed of all member states, sets policy and elects the Director-General
  • Six regional offices — India falls under WHO-SEARO (South-East Asia Regional Office)
  • WHO’s core functions extend beyond technical guidance: it also serves as the custodian of the binding international legal instrument covered next — the International Health Regulations

International Health Regulations (IHR 2005)

🟢 MUST KNOW

The IHR (2005) is a legally binding international instrument, covering all 194 WHO member states (196 States Parties in total).

Purpose: to prevent, protect against, control, and provide a public health response to the international spread of disease, in ways proportionate to public health risk — while avoiding unnecessary interference with international traffic and trade.

Core capacities countries must maintain:

  • Early warning and routine surveillance systems
  • Epidemiological and outbreak investigation skills
  • Laboratory expertise
  • Information and communication technology systems
  • Management systems, including a designated National IHR Focal Point for communicating with WHO

Direct link to Chapter 6: IHR is the legal framework requiring every country to be capable of the outbreak-investigation steps already covered there.

Global Health Financing and Delivery Actors

🟢 MUST KNOW

Beyond WHO, several other organizations shape global health outcomes:

  • UNICEF — child-focused UN agency; procures the large majority of vaccine commodities used in global immunization programmes
  • World Bank — a major financer of health systems globally, shaping health policy through lending and technical influence
  • The Global Fund (to Fight AIDS, Tuberculosis and Malaria) — responds to high-impact communicable diseases in resource-poor countries, including broader health-systems support
  • Gavi, the Vaccine Alliance — a public-private partnership (WHO, UNICEF, World Bank, vaccine industry, donor governments, civil society) that has helped immunize over 1.2 billion children, preventing more than 20.6 million deaths, and helped halve child mortality in 78 lower-income countries since 2000

Other UN and Partner Agencies

🟡 SHOULD KNOW

Beyond WHO/UNICEF/World Bank/Gavi, several further UN specialized agencies and external partners have shaped health outcomes in India and globally:

Agency Focus
UNDP (1966) Broad development financing/technical assistance across sectors, health included
UNFPA Population and reproductive health programming; assisting India since 1974
FAO (1945) Food production and nutrition; ran the World Freedom from Hunger Campaign (1960)
ILO (1919) Labour/occupational health standards — the International Labour Code

Bilateral and philanthropic contributors have also mattered historically: USAID (malaria eradication, family planning support), the Colombo Plan (helped establish AIIMS New Delhi), and the Rockefeller and Ford Foundations (medical education, rural health service pilots, and institutions including AIIMS and the National Institute of Virology, Pune).

Global Health Security

🟡 SHOULD KNOW

Global health security refers to defence against internal and external public health risks and threats — spanning protection against cross-border infectious disease spread and ensuring access to essential medicines and health services. The concept was formally linked to epidemic alert and response by a 2001 World Health Assembly resolution.

Connects directly to Chapter 6’s herd immunity and outbreak-control concepts — but scaled to the level of international cooperation rather than a single country’s response.

Health Diplomacy

🟡 SHOULD KNOW

Health diplomacy sits at the interface between international health assistance and international political relations — using negotiation, advocacy, and cross-sector collaboration (health and foreign affairs together) to advance global health goals. It’s a relatively young and still-evolving field within international relations theory, reflecting how health crises increasingly require coordinated action across national borders and sectors that traditionally operated separately.

Gavi’s Public-Private Partnership Model

🟣 MAY KNOW

Gavi’s structure — bringing together governments, WHO, UNICEF, the World Bank, vaccine manufacturers, and philanthropic funders (e.g. the Gates Foundation) under one financing and delivery mechanism — is often cited as a model for how global health initiatives can pool diverse stakeholders’ comparative advantages, beyond what any single international organization could achieve alone. Gavi also funds global vaccine stockpiles for diseases like Ebola, cholera, meningococcal disease, and yellow fever.

From National Program to International Architecture

🟣 MAY KNOW

This chapter completes a thread running through much of this curriculum: national programmes like Universal Immunization (Chapter 14), Ayushman Bharat (Chapter 11), and NSCAEM (Chapter 15) don’t operate in isolation — they exist within, and are often financially or technically supported by, this wider international architecture of WHO governance, binding regulation (IHR), and global financing partnerships (Gavi, Global Fund, World Bank).
