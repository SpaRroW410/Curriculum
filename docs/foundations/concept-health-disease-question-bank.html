Concept of Health and Disease — Question Bank

Concept of Health and Disease: Question Bank

A written companion to Chapter 2: Concept of Health and Disease for practice, organized to match the deck’s three lectures. Descriptive questions (long essay, short essay, short answer/notes, reasoning-type, case scenario) are for written practice — try them before checking anything. MCQs are followed by a lettered answer key; try them before checking too.

The interactive, auto-checked versions of these MCQs are also available as Self-Check slides within each lecture of the chapter deck itself.

Lecture 1: Concept of Health

Descriptive Questions

Long Essay

  1. Define health and discuss the different models/concepts of health — biomedical, ecological, psychosocial, and holistic.
  2. Discuss the concept of positive health and describe the various indicators used to measure the health status of a community.

Short Essay

  1. Describe the dimensions of health.
  2. Discuss the spectrum of health and the iceberg phenomenon of disease.
  3. Describe the determinants of health, including political determinants.
  4. Discuss PQLI and HDI as composite indices of development and health.

Short Answers / Notes

  1. Write short notes on: (a) Impairment, disability, and handicap (b) Well-being vs wellness (c) Relativeness of health.

Reasoning Type

  1. Why is health considered a multidimensional and dynamic concept rather than a static, purely biomedical one?
  2. Why are objective indicators of health alone considered insufficient to capture the true health status of a population?

Case Scenario

  1. A 45-year-old man from a low-income household reports feeling constantly fatigued, though clinical investigations show no diagnosable disease. He has been unable to work regularly and feels socially isolated.
    1. Is this individual “healthy” by the biomedical model? Justify.
    2. How would the WHO’s definition of health assess this individual?
    3. Which dimension(s) of health are primarily affected?
    4. Where would you place this individual on the health–disease spectrum?
    5. Suggest measures, drawing on the determinants of health, that could improve this individual’s health status.

Lecture 2: Concept of Disease and Prevention

Descriptive Questions

  1. Differentiate between disease, illness, and sickness, and explain the impairment-disability-handicap taxonomy with an example.
  2. Describe the natural history of disease and the iceberg phenomenon, using a disease of your choice as an example.
  3. Discuss the levels of prevention and the corresponding modes of intervention, applied across the individual, family, community, and system units.
  4. Differentiate between disease control, elimination, and eradication, giving one example of each.
  5. Discuss the epidemiological transition and the double/triple burden of disease in the Indian context.

Multiple Choice Questions

  1. A person feels unwell and is fatigued, but no clinical or laboratory abnormality can be found. This best describes:

    1. Disease
    2. Illness
    3. Sickness

    1. Agent
    2. Host

    1. Disease

  9. A patient has early-stage hypertension detectable only through blood pressure measurement, with no symptoms and normal daily functioning. This best illustrates:

    1. Impairment

  12. Genetic counselling offered to a couple before conception is an example of prevention applied at which unit?

    1. Individual
    2. Family
    3. Community

  13. Screening, case-finding, and prompt treatment to halt disease progression is the mode of intervention known as:

    1. Health promotion
    2. Specific protection
    3. Early diagnosis and treatment

  14. The permanent, worldwide reduction of a disease’s incidence to zero, such that intervention measures are no longer needed, is termed:

    1. Control
    2. Elimination
    3. Eradication
    4. Surveillance

  15. Surveillance based on a selected sample of reporting sites, rather than the whole population, is called:

    1. Monitoring
    2. Sentinel surveillance
    3. Evaluation of control
    4. Eradication

  16. India’s simultaneous experience of a continuing communicable-disease burden alongside a fast-rising non-communicable-disease burden is termed:

    1. Epidemiological transition
    2. Double burden of disease
    3. Triple burden
    4. Syndemic
