Man and Medicine: Question Bank

Question Bank: Man and Medicine

A written companion to Man and Medicine — short essay/short answer questions for written practice, and MCQs (Single Best Answer, Multiple Correct Answer, and Assertion–Reason) with an answer key for self-testing.

This is a growing resource — more chapters will be added here as they’re covered.

Chapter 1: Introduction to Community Medicine

Short Essay/Short Answer Questions (5–8 marks)

  1. Enumerate the objectives of community medicine.
  2. Why is community medicine considered essential for undergraduate medical training?
  3. Scope of community medicine.
  4. Role of community medicine in national health development.
  5. What do you mean by “de-professionalization of medicine”?
  6. What is the contribution to medical sciences by: (a) Robert Koch, (b) John Snow, (c) James Lind?

Multiple Choice Questions (1 mark)

A. Single Best Answer (SBA)

  1. Community medicine primarily differs from clinical medicine in that it focuses on:
    1. Individual patient care
    2. Hospital-based management
    3. Health of populations
    4. Curative services only
  2. The term “Community Medicine” emphasizes:
    1. Treatment of community-acquired infections
    2. Preventive, promotive, and social aspects of health
    3. Medical care in rural areas only
    4. Primary care practice
  3. A district health officer plans a program to reduce the burden of hypertension by promoting healthy diet, physical activity, tobacco cessation, and community awareness rather than focusing only on treatment of diagnosed patients. This approach best reflects the principles of:
    1. Clinical medicine
    2. Curative medicine
    3. Community medicine
    4. Hospital-based care
  4. During a village survey, medical students collect data on sanitation, drinking water, housing conditions, and socioeconomic status to assess disease patterns in the community. This activity highlights which core focus of community medicine?
    1. Individual patient diagnosis
    2. Hospital management of diseases
    3. Population-based approach to health
    4. Emergency medical services
  5. A medical college introduces field visits, family adoption programs, and community surveys as part of undergraduate teaching. The primary purpose of including these activities is to:
    1. Improve clinical diagnostic skills
    2. Train students in laboratory techniques
    3. Sensitize students to community health needs
    4. Reduce hospital workload
  6. A public health program focuses on improving nutrition, sanitation, education, and employment opportunities to achieve better health outcomes. This program reflects which broader role of community medicine?
    1. Disease treatment only
    2. National health development
    3. Emergency medical response
    4. Hospital administration
  7. Which of the following best reflects the scope of community medicine?
    1. Diagnosis and treatment
    2. Rehabilitation only
    3. Prevention, promotion, and protection of health
    4. Emergency care

B. Multiple Correct Answer (MCMA)

  1. Functions of community medicine include:
    1. Disease prevention
    2. Health promotion
    3. Social rehabilitation
    4. Individual surgical care
  2. Community medicine integrates knowledge from:
    1. Epidemiology
    2. Biostatistics
    3. Sociology
    4. Pathology only

C. Assertion–Reason (A-R)

  1. Assertion: Community medicine is considered a bridge between medicine and social sciences. Reason: It addresses social determinants affecting health and disease.
    1. Both A and R are true and R explains A
    2. Both A and R are true, but R does not explain A
    3. A is true, R is false
    4. A is false, R is true

Answers

  1. c 2. b 3. c 4. c 5. c 6. b 7. c 8. a–c 9. a–c 10. b

Chapter 2: Indigenous System of Medicine in India

Short Essay/Short Answer Questions (5–8 marks)

  1. List the basic principles of Ayurveda (only headings/broad points).
  2. Mention two principles, each of Unani and Homoeopathy.
  3. Write short points on the role of Yoga in health promotion.
  4. AYUSH in India: Public health relevance and integration (brief).
  5. Limitations/precautions in AYUSH use (standardization, safety, interactions, evidence).

Multiple Choice Questions (1 mark)

A. Single Best Answer (SBA)

  1. Which system is traditionally associated with the concept of “Tridosha” (Vata, Pitta, Kapha)?
    1. Unani
    2. Siddha
    3. Ayurveda
    4. Homoeopathy
  2. “Like cures like” is the fundamental principle of:
    1. Unani
    2. Yoga
    3. Siddha
    4. Homoeopathy
  3. A patient with chronic low back pain attends a wellness center where yoga-based exercises, lifestyle modification, and stress management are advised along with standard medical care. This approach represents:
    1. Replacement of modern medicine
    2. Exclusive alternative therapy
    3. Complementary integration of AYUSH
    4. Emergency medical management
  4. A primary health center offers Ayurveda-based dietary counseling and yoga sessions for patients with obesity and diabetes, while continuing standard allopathic treatment. This model best demonstrates:
    1. Parallel medical systems without interaction
    2. Evidence-based integration of AYUSH in public health
    3. Substitution of modern medicine
    4. Curative care only
  5. In public health integration, AYUSH is most appropriately used for:
    1. Replacement of allopathic emergency care
    2. Complementary and supportive care where evidence and safety permit
    3. Exclusive management of outbreaks
    4. Surgical management of trauma
  6. A patient develops liver toxicity after consuming an herbal preparation without standard labeling or dosage information. This situation highlights which limitation of AYUSH systems?
    1. Lack of trained practitioners
    2. Absence of preventive potential
    3. Issues of standardization and safety
    4. Ineffectiveness in chronic diseases
  7. A district health authority plans to use AYUSH practitioners mainly for health promotion, lifestyle counseling, and preventive care rather than acute emergencies. This decision is based on the understanding that AYUSH systems are best suited for:
    1. Trauma and surgical care
    2. Intensive care management
    3. Wellness and preventive health services
    4. Outbreak control

B. Multiple Correct Answer (MCMA)

  1. Potential public health contributions of AYUSH (when integrated appropriately) include:
    1. Health promotion and lifestyle modification
    2. Preventive counseling and wellness services
    3. Supportive care in chronic conditions (selected)
    4. Substitution for intensive care and emergency resuscitation

C. Assertion–Reason (A-R)

  1. Assertion: AYUSH integration in the health system can improve access to wellness and preventive services. Reason: AYUSH systems have structured approaches for lifestyle-based health promotion.
    1. Both A and R are true and R explains A
    2. Both A and R are true, but R does not explain A
    3. A is true, R is false
    4. A is false, R is true
  2. Assertion: AYUSH drugs are always safe because they are natural. Reason: Natural origin guarantees absence of adverse effects and interactions.
    1. Both A and R are true and R explains A
    2. Both A and R are true, but R does not explain A
    3. A is true, R is false
    4. A is false, R is true

Answers

  1. c 2. d 3. c 4. b 5. b 6. c 7. c 8. a–c 9. a 10. d

Historical Concepts in Community Medicine — Review Exercises

From a second source textbook’s chapter on the history of medicine and public health — pairs with the Five Eras of Medical History section of the chapter.

Multiple Choice Questions

  1. In the history of medicine, the period of 3000 BCE to 500 AD is known as:
    1. Prehistoric era
    2. Dark ages
    3. Historic era
    4. Era of renaissance
  2. According to Ayurveda, how many humors are there, a balance of which maintains health?
    1. 2
    2. 3
    3. 4
    4. 5
  3. Which of the following sequences is chronologically correct (from most ancient to most recent) regarding Indian sages associated with Ayurveda?
    1. Charaka, Sushruta, Atreya
    2. Atreya, Charaka, Sushruta
    3. Sushruta, Atreya, Charaka
    4. Atreya, Sushruta, Charaka
  4. The father of medicine came from which of the following civilizations?
    1. Indian
    2. Chinese
    3. Greek
    4. Roman
  5. The book On Airs, Waters and Places was authored by:
    1. Hippocrates
    2. Galen
    3. Rhazes
    4. Avicenna
  6. The middle (dark) ages saw the rise of which of the following systems of medicine?
    1. Ayurvedic
    2. Greek
    3. Egyptian
    4. Arabic
  7. The book Cannons of Avicenna forms the basis of practice of which of the following systems of medicine?
    1. Unani-Tibb
    2. Homeopathy
    3. Ayurveda-Siddha
    4. Allopathy
  8. Who is given the title of “Founder of Biostatistics”?
    1. Hippocrates
    2. Paracelsus
    3. Graunt
    4. Farr
  9. Which of the following was the first ever scientifically conducted clinical trial in medical history?
    1. Trial of citrus fruits by Lind for preventing scurvy
    2. Experiment on variolation by Jenner
    3. Trial of streptomycin for treatment of tuberculosis
    4. Trial of BCG vaccine
  10. Which of the following combinations of eminent founders of public health and the country they worked in is correct?
    1. Shattuck, UK
    2. Chadwick, USA
    3. Chadwick, UK
    4. Shattuck, Canada
  11. John Snow, who laid the foundations of the science of epidemic investigations, was, by profession:
    1. Epidemiologist
    2. Surgeon
    3. Lawyer
    4. Anesthesiologist
  12. The Registration of Births and Deaths Act was first passed in India in:
    1. 1859
    2. 1881
    3. 1901
    4. 1921
  13. In India, during the British rule, the transfer of sanitary and public health functions to the provinces (now called states) was recommended by:
    1. Warren Hastings Committee
    2. Plague Commission
    3. Montague-Chelmsford Committee
    4. Simon Commission
  14. Which of the following gave extensive recommendations regarding primary health-care during British-ruled India?
    1. Bhore Committee
    2. Alma-Ata Conference
    3. Both the above
    4. None of the above
  15. Dr. Sushila Nayyar was:
    1. A minister in central government
    2. An ardent follower of Mahatma Gandhi
    3. A medical teacher
    4. All of the above
  16. The world was declared free of smallpox in:
    1. 1973
    2. 1976
    3. 1980
    4. 1984
  17. India, in 1953, became the first country in the world to launch a:
    1. Tuberculosis control program
    2. Malaria control program
    3. Family planning program
    4. Maternal and child health program
  18. In India, the first ever National Health Policy was enunciated by the government in:
    1. 1971
    2. 1980
    3. 1982
    4. 2000
  19. The last indigenous case of polio in India occurred in:
    1. January 2011
    2. April 2009
    3. February 2014
    4. December 2015
  20. National Institution for Transforming India (NITI Aayog) is a body which has replaced the earlier:
    1. Medical Council of India
    2. Central Council of Health
    3. Indian Council of Medical Research
    4. Planning Commission
  21. How many persons in our country are likely to be covered by the National Health Protection Scheme (Ayushman Bharat)?
    1. 10 million
    2. 10 crore
    3. 50 crore
    4. 130 crore
  22. In India, “Doctors’ Day” is celebrated to commemorate the memory of:
    1. Dr Sushila Nayyar
    2. Dr B C Roy
    3. Sir Joseph Bhore
    4. Dr A L Mudaliar
  23. WHO declared COVID-19 as a Public Health Emergency of International Concern (PHEIC) on:
    1. 30 December 2019
    2. 30 January 2020
    3. 15 February 2020
    4. 25 March 2020

Answers

  1. c 2. b 3. d 4. c 5. a 6. d 7. a 8. c 9. a 10. c 11. d 12. b 13. c 14. a 15. d 16. c 17. c 18. c
  2. a 20. d 21. c 22. b 23. b
Note

Q9 and Q11 are worth knowing beyond the exam: James Lind’s 1747 citrus-fruit trial against scurvy is widely regarded as the first controlled clinical trial in medical history, and John Snow — best known for the 1854 Broad Street cholera investigation — was also a pioneering anesthesiologist (he administered chloroform to Queen Victoria during childbirth).

General Concepts in Health, Community Medicine and Public Health — Review Exercises

Pairs with the community-medicine definitions, Winslow’s public-health definition, Health for All, and Primary Health Care sections of Man and Medicine.

Multiple Choice Questions

  1. Which of the following is NOT a difference between curative medicine and preventive medicine?
    1. One focuses on the individual and the other on a group of people
    2. They use different types of clinical methods
    3. One focuses on the cure of disease and the other on prevention
    4. The results are apparent in one but not in the other
  2. The aspects of physical, mental, social, and spiritual well-being which are included in the WHO definition of health are termed:
    1. Dimensions
    2. Facets
    3. Subdomains
    4. All of the above
  3. In the WHO definition of health, which of the following components is NOT included but should be included?
    1. Physical
    2. Social
    3. Spiritual
    4. Mental
  4. The standard definition of public health was given by:
    1. Winslow
    2. Virchow
    3. Shattuck
    4. Chadwick
  5. The Alma-Ata declaration on health for all was made in which year?
    1. 1948
    2. 1971
    3. 1978
    4. 2000
  6. The revised target for HFA by the year 2020, in respect of maternal mortality ratio, is to bring it down to:
    1. <1 per 1000
    2. <3 per 1000
    3. <0.5 per 1000
    4. None of the above
  7. In the revised targets for health for all, which of the following indicators will be used to measure “health equity”?
    1. Eradication of measles
    2. Childhood stunting
    3. Access to safe water and sanitation
    4. Life expectancy at birth
  8. Provision of basic health-care at the village level by a trained village health guide (VHG) is an example of which of the following pillars of primary health-care?
    1. Community participation
    2. Equitable distribution
    3. Appropriate technology
    4. Intersectoral coordination
  9. The graded (tier) system of providing health-care to the community has how many tiers?
    1. 1
    2. 2
    3. 3
    4. 4
  10. Which of the following is NOT a characteristic of primary health-care?
    1. It is shaped around the life patterns of the population it serves
    2. It is focused around basic sanitation
    3. It gives priority to building state-of-the-art hospitals
    4. It has commitment to appropriate treatment of common diseases and injuries
  11. The target year for achieving the MDGs is:
    1. 2010
    2. 2015
    3. 2020
    4. 2025
  12. The most essential strategy for achieving health for all is:
    1. Appropriate health-related laws
    2. Adequate tertiary-care hospitals
    3. Primary health-care
    4. Safe water supply
  13. Millennium Development Goals have been laid down by:
    1. UNO
    2. WHO
    3. UNICEF
    4. Government of India
  14. The ICDS scheme is a very good example of:
    1. Intersectoral coordination
    2. Appropriate technology
    3. Epidemiological surveillance
    4. Philanthropic action
  15. Community participation, intersectoral coordination, equitable distribution, and appropriate technology are the four pillars of:
    1. The Planning Commission
    2. Primary health-care
    3. Universal health coverage
    4. The medical education system
  16. The WHO Day 2018 focused on:
    1. Primary health-care
    2. Universal health coverage
    3. Health for all
    4. Comprehensive health-care
  17. Which of the following was a pioneer of “social medicine”?
    1. Alfred Grotjahn
    2. Robert Koch
    3. Joseph Bhore
    4. C.E.A. Winslow
  18. The policy in which complete health-care is provided free, funded by the government, for all citizens of a country, is known as:
    1. Public health
    2. Social medicine
    3. Socialized medicine
    4. Preventive medicine
  19. “Appropriate treatment for common diseases and injuries” is a:
    1. Characteristic of PHC
    2. Component of PHC
    3. Pillar of PHC
    4. None of the above
  20. As per the original Alma-Ata declaration, “Health for All” was to be achieved by the year:
    1. 1978
    2. 1990
    3. 2000
    4. 2015

Answers

  1. b 2. c 3. d 4. a 5. c 6. a 7. b 8. a 9. c 10. c 11. b 12. c 13. a 14. a 15. b 16. b 17. a 18. c
  2. b 20. c
