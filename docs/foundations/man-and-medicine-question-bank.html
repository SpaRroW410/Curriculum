Man and Medicine: Question Bank

Question Bank: Man and Medicine

A written companion to Man and Medicine — short essay/short answer questions for written practice, and MCQs (Single Best Answer, Multiple Correct Answer, and Assertion–Reason) with an answer key for self-testing.

This is a growing resource — more chapters will be added here as they’re covered.

Chapter 1: Introduction to Community Medicine

Short Essay/Short Answer Questions (5–8 marks)

  1. Enumerate the objectives of community medicine.
  2. Why is community medicine considered essential for undergraduate medical training?
  3. Scope of community medicine.
  4. Role of community medicine in national health development.
  5. What do you mean by “de-professionalization of medicine”?
  6. What is the contribution to medical sciences by: (a) Robert Koch, (b) John Snow, (c) James Lind?

Multiple Choice Questions (1 mark)

A. Single Best Answer (SBA)

  1. Community medicine primarily differs from clinical medicine in that it focuses on:
    1. Individual patient care
    2. Hospital-based management
    3. Health of populations
    4. Curative services only
  2. The term “Community Medicine” emphasizes:
    1. Treatment of community-acquired infections
    2. Preventive, promotive, and social aspects of health
    3. Medical care in rural areas only
    4. Primary care practice
  3. A district health officer plans a program to reduce the burden of hypertension by promoting healthy diet, physical activity, tobacco cessation, and community awareness rather than focusing only on treatment of diagnosed patients. This approach best reflects the principles of:
    1. Clinical medicine
    2. Curative medicine
    3. Community medicine
    4. Hospital-based care
  4. During a village survey, medical students collect data on sanitation, drinking water, housing conditions, and socioeconomic status to assess disease patterns in the community. This activity highlights which core focus of community medicine?
    1. Individual patient diagnosis
    2. Hospital management of diseases
    3. Population-based approach to health
    4. Emergency medical services
  5. A medical college introduces field visits, family adoption programs, and community surveys as part of undergraduate teaching. The primary purpose of including these activities is to:
    1. Improve clinical diagnostic skills
    2. Train students in laboratory techniques
    3. Sensitize students to community health needs
    4. Reduce hospital workload
  6. A public health program focuses on improving nutrition, sanitation, education, and employment opportunities to achieve better health outcomes. This program reflects which broader role of community medicine?
    1. Disease treatment only
    2. National health development
    3. Emergency medical response
    4. Hospital administration
  7. Which of the following best reflects the scope of community medicine?
    1. Diagnosis and treatment
    2. Rehabilitation only
    3. Prevention, promotion, and protection of health
    4. Emergency care

B. Multiple Correct Answer (MCMA)

  1. Functions of community medicine include:
    1. Disease prevention
    2. Health promotion
    3. Social rehabilitation
    4. Individual surgical care
  2. Community medicine integrates knowledge from:
    1. Epidemiology
    2. Biostatistics
    3. Sociology
    4. Pathology only

C. Assertion–Reason (A-R)

  1. Assertion: Community medicine is considered a bridge between medicine and social sciences. Reason: It addresses social determinants affecting health and disease.
    1. Both A and R are true and R explains A
    2. Both A and R are true, but R does not explain A
    3. A is true, R is false
    4. A is false, R is true

Answers

  1. c 2. b 3. c 4. c 5. c 6. b 7. c 8. a–c 9. a–c 10. b

Chapter 2: Indigenous System of Medicine in India

Short Essay/Short Answer Questions (5–8 marks)

  1. List the basic principles of Ayurveda (only headings/broad points).
  2. Mention two principles, each of Unani and Homoeopathy.
  3. Write short points on the role of Yoga in health promotion.
  4. AYUSH in India: Public health relevance and integration (brief).
  5. Limitations/precautions in AYUSH use (standardization, safety, interactions, evidence).

Multiple Choice Questions (1 mark)

A. Single Best Answer (SBA)

  1. Which system is traditionally associated with the concept of “Tridosha” (Vata, Pitta, Kapha)?
    1. Unani
    2. Siddha
    3. Ayurveda
    4. Homoeopathy
  2. “Like cures like” is the fundamental principle of:
    1. Unani
    2. Yoga
    3. Siddha
    4. Homoeopathy
  3. A patient with chronic low back pain attends a wellness center where yoga-based exercises, lifestyle modification, and stress management are advised along with standard medical care. This approach represents:
    1. Replacement of modern medicine
    2. Exclusive alternative therapy
    3. Complementary integration of AYUSH
    4. Emergency medical management
  4. A primary health center offers Ayurveda-based dietary counseling and yoga sessions for patients with obesity and diabetes, while continuing standard allopathic treatment. This model best demonstrates:
    1. Parallel medical systems without interaction
    2. Evidence-based integration of AYUSH in public health
    3. Substitution of modern medicine
    4. Curative care only
  5. In public health integration, AYUSH is most appropriately used for:
    1. Replacement of allopathic emergency care
    2. Complementary and supportive care where evidence and safety permit
    3. Exclusive management of outbreaks
    4. Surgical management of trauma
  6. A patient develops liver toxicity after consuming an herbal preparation without standard labeling or dosage information. This situation highlights which limitation of AYUSH systems?
    1. Lack of trained practitioners
    2. Absence of preventive potential
    3. Issues of standardization and safety
    4. Ineffectiveness in chronic diseases
  7. A district health authority plans to use AYUSH practitioners mainly for health promotion, lifestyle counseling, and preventive care rather than acute emergencies. This decision is based on the understanding that AYUSH systems are best suited for:
    1. Trauma and surgical care
    2. Intensive care management
    3. Wellness and preventive health services
    4. Outbreak control

B. Multiple Correct Answer (MCMA)

  1. Potential public health contributions of AYUSH (when integrated appropriately) include:
    1. Health promotion and lifestyle modification
    2. Preventive counseling and wellness services
    3. Supportive care in chronic conditions (selected)
    4. Substitution for intensive care and emergency resuscitation

C. Assertion–Reason (A-R)

  1. Assertion: AYUSH integration in the health system can improve access to wellness and preventive services. Reason: AYUSH systems have structured approaches for lifestyle-based health promotion.
    1. Both A and R are true and R explains A
    2. Both A and R are true, but R does not explain A
    3. A is true, R is false
    4. A is false, R is true
  2. Assertion: AYUSH drugs are always safe because they are natural. Reason: Natural origin guarantees absence of adverse effects and interactions.
    1. Both A and R are true and R explains A
    2. Both A and R are true, but R does not explain A
    3. A is true, R is false
    4. A is false, R is true

Answers

  1. c 2. d 3. c 4. b 5. b 6. c 7. c 8. a–c 9. a 10. c
