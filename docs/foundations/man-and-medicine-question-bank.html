Man and Medicine: Question Bank

A written companion to Chapter 1: Man and Medicine for practice. Short essay/short answer questions are for written practice — a model-answer key with key points is given at the end of each chapter’s section (not full essays). MCQs are followed by a lettered answer key; try them before checking.

The interactive, auto-checked versions of these MCQs are also available as Self-Check slides within the chapter deck itself.

Chapter 1: Introduction to Community Medicine

Short Essay / Short Answer Questions

  1. Define Community Medicine. Discuss its scope and how it differs from clinical/hospital medicine.
  2. Differentiate between Hygiene, Preventive Medicine, Social Medicine, and Community Medicine.
  3. Describe the steps involved in community diagnosis, along with the parameters used to assess a health programme.
  4. Discuss the WHO’s definition of public health and its core functions.
  5. Write short notes on: (a) Social anatomy, physiology, pathology, and therapy of a community (b) Community Medicine vs Family Medicine.

Multiple Choice Questions

A. Single Best Answer (SBA)

  1. Community medicine primarily differs from clinical medicine in that it focuses on:

    1. Individual patient care
    2. Hospital-based management
    3. Health of populations
    4. Curative services only

  2. Which of the following best reflects the scope of community medicine?

    1. Diagnosis and treatment
    2. Rehabilitation only
    3. Prevention, promotion, and protection of health
    4. Emergency care

  3. The term “Community Medicine” places its central emphasis on:

    1. Diagnosis and treatment of individual patients
    2. The community/population as the unit of care, not the individual
    3. Advanced laboratory investigations
    4. Hospital bed-occupancy management

  4. A district health officer, on noticing a cluster of diarrhoeal cases in a village, organizes house-to-house surveys, tests drinking-water sources, and conducts health education sessions. This set of activities best illustrates:

    1. Clinical case management
    2. Applied community medicine practice in the field
    3. Hospital infection control
    4. Individual patient counselling

  5. Before planning any intervention, medical students conduct a village health survey to identify the prevailing health problems and locally available resources. This activity is an example of:

    1. A clinical audit
    2. Community diagnosis
    3. A case-control study
    4. A hospital needs-assessment

  6. The primary purpose of field visits during the Community Medicine posting is to:

    1. Fulfil attendance requirements only
    2. Study health problems and services in their real community setting
    3. Practice surgical procedures
    4. Conduct hospital ward rounds

  7. Community Medicine contributes most directly to national health development by:

    1. Running private specialty clinics
    2. Planning, implementing, and evaluating public health programmes
    3. Publishing case reports only
    4. Training only surgical residents

B. Multiple Correct Answer (MCMA) — select all that apply

  1. Which of the following are recognized functions of Community Medicine?

    1. Prevention and control of disease in populations
    2. Marketing pharmaceutical products
    3. Promotion of health through education and intersectoral action
    4. Maximizing hospital revenue

  2. Community Medicine integrates knowledge and methods from which of the following disciplines?

    1. Epidemiology and biostatistics
    2. Social and behavioural sciences
    3. Corporate financial accounting
    4. Automotive engineering

C. Assertion–Reason (A-R)

  1. Assertion (A): Community Medicine serves as a bridge between clinical medicine and the social sciences. Reason (R): The WHO was founded in 1948 with its headquarters in Geneva.

    1. A is true, R is false
    2. A is false, R is true
    3. Both A and R are true, but R is NOT the correct explanation of A
    4. Both A and R are false

Answers

Short Essay / Short Answer — key points:

  1. Community Medicine = the branch of medicine concerned with the health of populations rather than individuals; integrates preventive, promotive, curative, and rehabilitative care; scope spans community diagnosis, epidemiology, health administration, and public health programmes; differs from clinical/hospital medicine chiefly in its unit of concern (community vs individual patient).
  2. Hygiene = personal cleanliness practices; Preventive Medicine = averting disease in individuals/high-risk groups; Social Medicine = studying disease in its social context; Community Medicine = the broadest, population-level integration of all of the above with health administration.
  3. Community diagnosis steps: (i) define the community, (ii) collect data on health status and determinants, (iii) analyze and identify problems, (iv) prioritize problems, (v) plan action. Assessment parameters: relevance, adequacy, accessibility, availability, affordability, completeness.
  4. WHO’s public health definition (Winslow, 1920): the science and art of preventing disease, prolonging life, and promoting health through organized community effort. Core functions: assessment, policy development, and assurance (and their component activities — surveillance, health promotion, regulation, service provision, workforce development, etc.).
    1. Social anatomy/physiology/pathology/therapy — treating the community as an organism whose structure, functioning, ill-health, and treatment can each be studied, analogous to an individual patient. (b) Community Medicine works at the population level through organized public programmes; Family Medicine delivers comprehensive, continuous, individual/family-level primary care.

Chapter 2: Indigenous System of Medicine in India (AYUSH)

Short Essay / Short Answer Questions

  1. Describe the AYUSH systems of medicine in India and discuss their role and limitations in public health.
  2. Write short notes on: (a) Tridosha theory of Ayurveda (b) The fundamental principle of Homeopathy (c) Unani and Siddha systems of medicine.
  3. Discuss how AYUSH systems can be integrated into primary health care, with examples.

Multiple Choice Questions

A. Single Best Answer (SBA)

  1. The traditional concept of “Tridosha” — Vata, Pitta, and Kapha — is associated with which system of medicine?

    1. Unani
    2. Siddha
    3. Ayurveda
    4. Homeopathy

  2. “Like cures like” is the fundamental principle of:

    1. Unani
    2. Yoga
    3. Siddha
    4. Homeopathy

  3. A patient develops liver toxicity after taking an unlabeled herbal preparation of unclear dosage. This best illustrates which limitation of AYUSH systems?

    1. Lack of trained practitioners
    2. Absence of preventive potential
    3. Issues of standardization and safety
    4. Ineffectiveness in chronic diseases

  4. A corporate wellness programme uses regular yoga and meditation sessions to reduce employee stress and improve mental well-being. This approach is best classified under which AYUSH system?

    1. Unani
    2. Siddha
    3. Yoga and Naturopathy
    4. Homeopathy

  5. A rural primary health centre incorporates Ayurvedic physicians to manage chronic, non-emergency conditions alongside allopathic care. This reflects which model of health-care delivery?

    1. Exclusive allopathic model
    2. An integrative AYUSH–allopathy model at the primary care level
    3. Tertiary referral model
    4. Purely preventive model with no curative component

  6. AYUSH systems of medicine are, in general, most appropriately used for:

    1. Emergency trauma surgery
    2. Acute, life-threatening infections requiring intensive care
    3. Chronic conditions, wellness promotion, and preventive care
    4. Organ transplantation

  7. Which of the following best describes the primary public-health role of AYUSH systems today?

    1. Replacing allopathic emergency medicine entirely
    2. Complementing allopathy through wellness, prevention, and chronic-care support
    3. Providing the sole basis for national immunization programmes
    4. Serving only as a historical/cultural reference with no active clinical role

B. Multiple Correct Answer (MCMA) — select all that apply

  1. Which of the following are potential public-health contributions of AYUSH systems?

    1. Low-cost, accessible chronic-disease and wellness care
    2. Promotion of healthy lifestyle practices — yoga, diet, daily regimen
    3. Definitive management of major trauma
    4. Replacement of vaccination programmes

C. Assertion–Reason (A-R)

  1. Assertion (A): Integrating AYUSH practitioners into the primary health-care workforce can improve access to care in underserved areas. Reason (R): India has a large trained AYUSH workforce that can be deployed to supplement allopathic human resources for health.

    1. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
    2. Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A
    3. A is true, R is false
    4. A is false, R is true

  2. Assertion (A): All AYUSH medicines are inherently safe because they are derived from natural sources. Reason (R): Herbal and natural products can still cause toxicity, especially with poor standardization, incorrect dosage, or drug interactions.

    1. Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
    2. Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A
    3. A is true, R is false
    4. A is false, R is true

Answers

Short Essay / Short Answer — key points:

  1. AYUSH = Ayurveda, Yoga & Naturopathy, Unani, Siddha, Homeopathy, under the Ministry of AYUSH (2014). Role: low-cost, accessible wellness, prevention, and chronic-disease care, often integrated at the primary-care level. Limitations: variable standardization, safety/quality-control concerns, limited robust evidence base for many indications, unsuited to emergencies/surgical/critical care.
    1. Tridosha — Vata, Pitta, Kapha — the three fundamental humors of Ayurveda whose balance determines health, laid down in the Atharvaveda. (b) Homeopathy’s principle: “like cures like” (Samuel Hahnemann, 1796) — a substance causing symptoms in a healthy person treats similar symptoms when highly diluted. (c) Unani — Greco-Arabic system (Avicenna’s Canon of Medicine) based on four humors; Siddha — Tamil-origin system based on the five-element (Panchabhoota) theory.
  3. Integration examples: co-locating AYUSH practitioners at PHCs/sub-centres for chronic disease and wellness care; yoga/naturopathy in NCD prevention programmes; AYUSH workforce deployed to extend primary-care reach in underserved areas — all under the National AYUSH Mission.
