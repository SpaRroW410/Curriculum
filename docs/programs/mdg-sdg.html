The Eight Millennium Development Goals (2000–2015)

🟢 MUST KNOW

  1. Eradicate extreme poverty and hunger
  2. Achieve universal primary education
  3. Promote gender equality and empower women
  4. Reduce child mortality
  5. Improve maternal health
  6. Combat HIV/AIDS, malaria, and other diseases
  7. Ensure environmental sustainability
  8. Develop a global partnership for development

Three goals were explicitly health-focused: Goals 4, 5, and 6 — though several others (poverty/hunger, environment, global partnership) were also tracked using health-related indicators.

Key MDG Health Targets

🟢 MUST KNOW

  • Goal 4 (child mortality): reduce the under-five mortality rate by two-thirds between 1990 and 2015
  • Goal 6 (HIV/AIDS, malaria, other diseases): halt and begin to reverse the spread of HIV/AIDS by 2015; achieve universal access to HIV treatment by 2010; halt and begin to reverse malaria and other major diseases by 2015

Result: the MDGs galvanized unprecedented global attention, funding, and accountability on this focused set of concerns, with substantial — though incomplete — progress by 2015.

The Seventeen Sustainable Development Goals (2015–2030)

🟢 MUST KNOW

Adopted by all UN member states in September 2015, the SDGs comprise 17 goals and 169 targets, applying universally to all countries (not only low- and middle-income countries, as the MDGs largely did).

Health sits at SDG 3: Good Health and Well-being — “ensure healthy lives and promote well-being for all at all ages” — with 13 targets covering a much broader spectrum than the three MDG health goals.

Key SDG 3 Targets

🟢 MUST KNOW

  • Maternal mortality: reduce the global ratio to below 70 per 100,000 live births by 2030
  • Child mortality: end preventable newborn/under-5 deaths — targets of ≤12 neonatal deaths per 1,000 and ≤25 under-5 deaths per 1,000 live births
  • Communicable disease: end the epidemics of AIDS, tuberculosis, malaria, and neglected tropical diseases, and combat hepatitis and water-borne disease
  • SDG 3.4: reduce premature NCD mortality by one-third, and promote mental health (introduced already in the NCD chapter)
  • Universal Health Coverage (UHC) and access to safe, effective medicines and vaccines for all

Why the Agenda Broadened

🟡 SHOULD KNOW

MDGs SDGs
Scope 8 goals, narrowly focused 17 goals, 169 targets, broad
Applies to Primarily developing countries Universally, all countries
Process Perceived as top-down (set by experts/UN) Developed through inclusive, participatory consultation
Health coverage 3 goals: child, maternal, infectious disease 1 goal (SDG 3), but covering both communicable AND non-communicable disease, injuries, and UHC

The shift reflects a recognition that health outcomes depend on interlocking sectors (poverty, education, environment, inequality) — consistent with the multifactorial-causation and social-determinants ideas from earlier chapters.

Criticism of the SDG Approach

🟡 SHOULD KNOW

The SDGs’ breadth is also their most common criticism: with 17 goals and 169 targets, analysts argue the framework is less focused and harder to measure than the MDGs, risking diffused attention and resources across too many priorities at once — a direct trade-off against the MDGs’ narrower, more trackable design.

The Paradigm-Shift Challenges

🟣 MAY KNOW

Achieving the SDG 3 health agenda has been described as requiring a genuine paradigm shift across five challenges: intersectoral leadership on the structural drivers of health, shifting focus from treatment to prevention, addressing commercial determinants of ill-health (e.g. tobacco, alcohol, ultra-processed food industries), integrating rights-based approaches, and strengthening civic engagement and accountability.

India’s MDG-to-SDG Transition

🟣 MAY KNOW

India, alongside other countries, undertook a formal national consultation process on transitioning its own monitoring and planning systems from MDG to SDG indicators — reflecting that the SDGs, unlike the MDGs, require every country (not only lower-income ones) to track and report progress against the same global framework.
