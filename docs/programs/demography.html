This chapter gives the demographic vocabulary that underlies population- level health planning, and traces India’s role as a pioneer in national family planning policy.

The Demographic Transition Model

🟢 MUST KNOW

A generalized description of how birth rates, death rates, and population growth change as a society moves from pre-industrial to industrialized, in four stages:

  1. Stage 1 — High birth rate, high death rate → slow/stable growth (pre-industrial)
  2. Stage 2 — Death rate falls (better sanitation/medicine), birth rate stays high → rapid population growth
  3. Stage 3 — Birth rate begins to fall (urbanization, education, contraception) → growth slows
  4. Stage 4 — Low birth rate, low death rate → stable population

Where most transitions stall or reverse today: some countries now describe a Stage 5, where birth rate falls below death rate, causing population decline — not part of the classical four-stage model but increasingly relevant.

Crude Birth Rate and Crude Death Rate

🟢 MUST KNOW

Crude Birth Rate (CBR) — live births per 1,000 mid-year population in a given year.

Crude Death Rate (CDR) — deaths per 1,000 mid-year population in a given year.

Both are “crude” because they don’t adjust for the population’s age or sex structure — a country with an older population will show a higher CDR even with excellent health care, simply due to demographics.

Total Fertility Rate

🟢 MUST KNOW

Total Fertility Rate (TFR) — the average number of children a woman would bear over her lifetime, if she experienced the current age-specific fertility rates throughout her childbearing years.

Replacement-level fertility — the TFR at which a population exactly replaces itself across generations (approximately 2.1 in most settings, accounting for child mortality and the sex ratio at birth). A country with TFR consistently below this level will eventually see its population decline, absent migration.

Gross and Net Reproduction Rate

🟢 MUST KNOW

  • Gross Reproduction Rate (GRR) — like TFR, but counts only daughters a woman would have — a measure of generational replacement through the female line, ignoring mortality
  • Net Reproduction Rate (NRR) — refines GRR further by also adjusting for the probability that daughters survive to their mother’s childbearing age; NRR = 1 means the population is exactly replacing itself generation to generation

Exam tip: GRR ignores mortality; NRR accounts for it — this is the key distinguishing feature between the two.

India’s Family Planning Programme: Origins

🟡 SHOULD KNOW

In 1952, India became the first country in the world to launch an official national family planning programme, aiming to reduce birth rates “to stabilize the population at a level consistent with the requirements of the national economy.”

  • 1966 — a separate Department of Family Planning created within the Ministry of Health
  • 1977 — following the coercive sterilization controversies of the Emergency period, the new government’s population policy explicitly ruled out compulsion, making the programme purely voluntary — a landmark and lasting policy shift

National Population Policy 2000

🟡 SHOULD KNOW

India’s National Population Policy (NPP) 2000 set its goals around:

  • Addressing unmet need for contraception
  • Providing free and compulsory school education up to age 14
  • Reducing infant and maternal mortality
  • Achieving population stabilization by 2045

NPP 2000 implementation was later integrated with the National Rural Health Mission (launched 2005) as the operational vehicle for delivering its goals, connecting demography policy directly to the health-systems architecture from Chapter 11.

The MTP Act: Gestational Limits

🟡 SHOULD KNOW

The Medical Termination of Pregnancy (MTP) Act governs legally permitted induced abortion in India — “MTP” refers only to this legal category, distinct from spontaneous abortion (miscarriage).

Gestational age Requirement
Up to 20 weeks Opinion of one Registered Medical Practitioner
20–24 weeks Opinion of two RMPs, for specified eligible categories only (e.g. rape survivors, minors, change in marital status during pregnancy, disability)
Beyond 24 weeks Only for substantial fetal abnormality, as diagnosed by a Medical Board

Recognized indications: therapeutic (risk to mother’s life), eugenic (fetal abnormality), humanitarian (rape/incest), and social (contraceptive failure causing mental distress) — with the underlying public health goal of reducing unsafe abortion and its complications (haemorrhage, sepsis, infertility).

Consent: an adult woman’s own written consent is sufficient; a minor or a woman with mental illness requires guardian consent. The Act mandates confidentiality of the woman’s identity.

Age Structure and the Population Pyramid

🟣 MAY KNOW

A population pyramid — a bar chart of population by age and sex — visually reflects a country’s demographic-transition stage: a wide base (many young people) indicates high fertility (early transition), while a more rectangular shape indicates low fertility and an ageing population (late transition). India’s own age structure varies significantly by state, reflecting different transition stages within one country.

Demographic Dividend

🟣 MAY KNOW

The demographic dividend is the potential economic growth benefit that can arise when a country’s working-age population (15–64) is proportionally larger than its dependent population (children and elderly) — typically occurring during the middle stages of the demographic transition, as fertility falls but before the population ages substantially. Realizing this dividend depends on adequate education, employment, and health investment during that window.
