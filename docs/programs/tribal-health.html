Health Determinants Specific to Tribal Populations

🟢 MUST KNOW

India’s Scheduled Tribes (Adivasi communities) — about 8.6% of India’s population — face a distinct combination of health determinants:

  • Geographic isolation — habitations scattered across large areas with poor connectivity, transport, and communication
  • Socio-economic disadvantage — disproportionately represented in the poorest wealth deciles
  • Undernutrition and anaemia — prevailing at higher rates than the general population (Chapter 9’s malnutrition framework applies directly here)
  • Genetic disorders — notably sickle cell disease and G-6-PD deficiency, concentrated in specific tribal populations due to historical patterns of inheritance and marriage within communities

Sickle Cell Disease Burden

🟢 MUST KNOW

Sickle cell disease is disproportionately concentrated in India’s tribal population:

  • Roughly 73% of Indians carrying the sickle haemoglobin gene (HbS) are from tribal communities — despite tribes being only ~8.6% of the population
  • Prevalence of the sickle cell trait/disease varies widely by region and community (roughly 1–40% trait prevalence in different populations)
  • Highest burden concentrated in states including Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Odisha, and Assam

Why this matters for prevention: because carrier/disease status can be identified through a simple test, sickle cell disease is a strong candidate for secondary prevention (early detection) at population scale (Chapter 2’s levels of prevention).

National Sickle Cell Anaemia Elimination Mission (NSCAEM)

🟢 MUST KNOW

Announced in the 2023–24 Union Budget and launched by the Prime Minister on 1 July 2023 at Shahdol, Madhya Pradesh:

  • Goal: eliminate sickle cell disease as a public health problem in India by 2047
  • Target: universal screening of 7 crore (70 million) people aged 0–40 in affected tribal areas
  • Provides awareness generation, counselling, and access to affordable, quality care for those diagnosed — through collaboration between central ministries and state governments

This is a direct, named example of a vertical, disease-specific programme (Chapter 11’s terminology) targeted precisely because of the population-specific genetic burden described above.

Healthcare Infrastructure Gaps

🟢 MUST KNOW

Public health infrastructure in tribal regions is documented to be inadequate, understaffed, and underutilized, with a nationwide shortfall (relative to population-based norms) of thousands of Health Sub-Centres, Primary Health Centres, and Community Health Centres in tribal areas specifically.

Consequence: even where national programmes (NHM, Ayushman Bharat, NSCAEM) exist on paper, their reach into tribal areas is constrained by this underlying infrastructure gap — a reminder that programme design (Chapter 11) and programme reach are separate problems.

Expert Committee on Tribal Health

🟡 SHOULD KNOW

A four-year study led by Dr. Abhay Bang produced India’s first comprehensive tribal health report, “Tribal Health in India — Bridging the Gap and a Roadmap for the Future,” examining health status, cultural context, and healthcare infrastructure in tribal areas.

Key recommendation theme: addressing the underlying social determinants of tribal health (poverty, education, resource access) is as important as expanding clinical service delivery alone — echoing the social-determinants framing from Chapter 7’s discussion of the social gradient of health.

Access Barriers Beyond Infrastructure

🟡 SHOULD KNOW

The 2014 Xaxa Commission report identified additional non- infrastructure barriers specific to tribal communities:

  • Geographical isolation tied to remote/traditional living patterns
  • Lack of awareness of available health services
  • Cultural norms and beliefs that may not align with mainstream health-system practices, requiring culturally sensitive outreach rather than a one-size-fits-all delivery model

Digital-First Screening at Scale

🟣 MAY KNOW

NSCAEM has been noted for a digital-first approach — using digital tools to track screening, testing, and follow-up for a screening programme covering tens of millions of people across geographically dispersed and hard-to-reach tribal areas — an operational adaptation specifically shaped by the geographic-isolation challenge described earlier.

Particularly Vulnerable Tribal Groups (PVTGs)

🟣 MAY KNOW

Within India’s broader Scheduled Tribe population, the government designates certain groups as Particularly Vulnerable Tribal Groups (PVTGs) — communities with pre-agricultural technology, low literacy, declining or stagnant population, and greater isolation than tribal populations generally — requiring even more targeted policy attention than the general tribal-health framework.
